\section{Experimental Setup and Test Environment}
To validate the reliability, analytical accuracy, and computational efficiency of PricePulse BD, we conducted systematic experiments across three dimensions:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Linguistic Normalization and Taxonomy Precision:} Assessing the ability of the Unicode NFKC and alias matching engine to correctly resolve noisy regional dialectical variations and unit specifications.
    \item \textbf{Statistical Anomaly Detection Performance:} Evaluating the Compound Decision Rule on continuous historical price series embedded with verified supply shocks.
    \item \textbf{System Throughput and Latency Profiling:} Measuring REST API response times under concurrent load on commodity hardware.
\end{enumerate}

All benchmarks were executed on an off-the-shelf workstation configured with an 8-core AMD Ryzen processor, 16 GB DDR4 RAM, and an NVMe SSD running 64-bit Windows 11 with Python 3.14.7, SQLite 3.45 with WAL mode, and Node.js v24.19.0.

\section{Bilingual Normalization and Entity Resolution Accuracy}
We compiled an evaluation testbed containing 120 raw commodity strings extracted from archival DAM bulletins, retail e-grocery product lists (Chaldal, Shwapno), and unconstrained user search logs. The testbed contained four categories:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Exact Bengali Matches:} Canonical Unicode strings (e.g., ``দেশি পেঁয়াজ'', ``মোটা চাল'').
    \item \textbf{Dialectical / Phonetic Variants:} Transliterated or colloquial spellings (e.g., ``Peyaj Deshi'', ``mota chaul'', ``Alu Diamond'').
    \item \textbf{Complex Packaged Expressions:} Multi-word strings with packaging and unit descriptors (e.g., ``Teer Fortified Soybean Oil 5L Bottle'', ``Fresh Onion 1kg Net Bag'').
    \item \textbf{Negative / Out-of-Domain Tokens:} Non-commodity agricultural inputs (e.g., ``Urea Fertilizer'', ``Plastic Storage Box'').
\end{itemize}

\section{Anomaly Detection Validation: The 30-Day Onion Price Shock}
To evaluate the statistical anomaly engine, we simulated a realistic 30-day market sequence across four core essential commodities: Coarse Rice, Local Onion, Potato, and Soybean Oil. For the first 25 days, commodities followed stationary Brownian price walks with Gaussian noise ($\mathcal{N}(0, 1.2)$) reflecting normal trading variances.

On Day 26, a simulated supply shock was injected into the Local Onion price series—modeling a sudden cross-border export duty hike. The price rose sharply from an equilibrium of 65.0 BDT/kg to 98.0 BDT/kg over a 48-hour window (+50.7\% relative deviation).

Figure~\ref{fig:onion_shock} depicts the historical price trajectory plotted against the 14-day Rolling SMA baseline and standard deviation confidence intervals.

Immediately upon the onset of the supply shock on Day 26, the engine flagged a \textbf{Critical Spike}, generating the complete mathematical decomposition and auditable natural language justification without user intervention.

\section{Android Client Room Persistence \& Canvas Frame-Rate Benchmarking}
We benchmarked the native Android client on an Android 14 physical device (8-core ARM64, 8 GB RAM):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Offline Room Cold-Start Query:} Retrieving the complete 21-commodity catalog and active anomaly alerts from Room SQLite cache executed in $14.2 \pm 2.1\text{ ms}$, ensuring zero-latency startup without network connectivity.
    \item \textbf{Custom Compose Canvas Rendering:} Drawing the cubic bezier curve, dotted 14-day rolling SMA baseline, glowing anomaly markers, and touch-drag crosshair achieved sustained \textbf{118.4 FPS} on a 120Hz display with zero dropped frames.
    \item \textbf{Memory Overhead:} Peak heap footprint remained under 38 MB during continuous touch interaction and drag gestures.
\end{itemize}
