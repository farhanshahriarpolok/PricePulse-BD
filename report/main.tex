\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
Agricultural and retail essential commodity markets in developing economies such as Bangladesh are characterized by high price volatility, geographical fragmentation, asymmetric price transmission between intermediaries, and an absence of unified real-time monitoring infrastructure. Official market bulletins published by government agencies such as the Department of Agricultural Marketing (DAM) and Trading Corporation of Bangladesh (TCB) rely on disparate PDF/HTML bulletins and regional customary units (maund, seer, hali) that preclude direct analytical querying. Conversely, commercial online retail aggregators publish localized packet-level quotes lacking wholesale cross-referencing.

This research presents \textbf{PricePulse BD}, a location-aware, multi-source market price intelligence and explainable anomaly detection platform designed specifically for the sovereign economic context of Bangladesh. The system introduces: (1) a bilingual lexical normalization pipeline utilizing Unicode NFKC standardization and phonetic alias mapping for seamless Bengali-English commodity resolution; (2) a deterministic unit standardizer translating non-metric customary trade units to base SI metrics; (3) a four-factor linear confidence scoring model assessing observation provenance; (4) an explainable parametric anomaly detection engine combining Rolling Simple Moving Averages (14-day SMA), sample standard deviations, Z-scores, Volatility Coefficients of Variation (CV\%), and deterministic natural language explanation generators; and (5) a spatial spread layer computing inter-district markups rendered via GeoJSON feature collections. 

The software architecture leverages a decoupled, local-first paradigm with SQLite Write-Ahead Logging (WAL) concurrency, a high-throughput FastAPI REST engine, and a reactive dashboard client built with React, Vite, Leaflet, and Recharts. Empirical evaluation demonstrates sub-45ms P95 query latencies, 100\% precision on canonical taxonomy mapping across regional dialectical fixtures, and reliable identification of supply-shock price spikes without the opacity or computational overhead of black-box deep learning models.
